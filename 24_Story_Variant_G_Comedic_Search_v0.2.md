# Story Variant G v0.2 - The Search Party

Status: DESIGNED
Tone: Israeli adult comedy with emotional warmth
Target runtime: 5-7 minutes

## Logline

When a friend stops answering messages and calls for several days, one worried man sets out to find him. Every friend he visits joins the search, until the entire neighborhood is looking for the missing man, who is eventually discovered hosting an after-party in his own apartment with people he met the night before.

## Why this is the stronger direction

It preserves the recognizable emotional machinery of "Adon Shoko": preparation, a visit to a friend, another friend joining, repeated doors and a growing walking group. It then turns the repetition into a specifically Israeli comedy about worry, gossip, overreaction, hospitality and people who cannot leave a situation without adding one more person.

## Story

### Opening - The unanswered phone

CHAR_001 sends a polite message to CHAR_002. No reply. He calls. The phone rings once and goes to voicemail. A few days pass through quick inserts: unread messages, a blue check that never appears, an untouched coffee and CHAR_001 checking the same contact again.

He dresses carefully, takes his hat and walking stick, and goes to ask a friend in person.

### The chain begins

At the first apartment, the friend says CHAR_002 was probably at the cafe. He joins the search, but first insists on bringing back a borrowed serving dish. At the cafe, the waiter remembers seeing CHAR_002, but only after everyone argues about what time it was. A third friend joins because he has the spare key, although he cannot find it.

### The search becomes a social event

The group grows as they visit apartments, a kiosk, a courtyard and a corner cafe. Every person contributes a theory:

- he ran away to Eilat
- he is angry at someone
- he joined a meditation retreat
- his phone fell behind the washing machine
- he is probably asleep and should not be disturbed

They take the search very seriously while constantly stopping to eat, drink coffee and tell unrelated stories about CHAR_002. The humor comes from confident speculation, small arguments and the Israeli instinct to turn an emergency into a group outing.

### The serious turn

As evening becomes night, CHAR_001 admits that he is genuinely afraid. CHAR_002 has never disappeared like this before. The group goes quiet for a moment. They decide to check his apartment one last time.

### The reveal

They arrive at CHAR_002's building near dawn. Music and laughter are coming from inside. The door opens. CHAR_002 is alive, exhausted, barefoot and wearing yesterday's clothes. Behind him is a crowded after-party: strangers he met the night before, a borrowed speaker, empty cups and someone asleep in a bathtub.

He had returned home, put his phone on silent and invited everyone upstairs. He never noticed the search.

### Ending

The group is furious for exactly three seconds. Then someone asks whether there is coffee. CHAR_002 apologizes and invites them in. CHAR_001 looks at the exhausted crowd, smiles despite himself, and says they are staying only for five minutes.

Cut to a later morning image: the entire search party asleep in impossible positions around the apartment while CHAR_001 quietly polishes his shoes again. His phone buzzes. A new message arrives from another friend: "Where are you? We are looking for you."

## Moral

The moral is not "friends should not worry." It is:

**Friendship is wonderfully irrational: people may overreact, gossip and turn a simple search into chaos, but they still show up. The real danger is not being surrounded by noisy friends; it is assuming silence means you do not matter.**

The final message creates a comic loop while showing that the group has become a community.

## Recognition signals from the source structure

- careful morning preparation
- hat and walking stick as silhouette anchors
- a first visit that leads to another visit
- houses, doors and bell rings
- a growing group walking together
- repetition that changes meaning each time

The story remains original in character motivation, dialogue, setting, conflict and ending. It does not use the original song's words, melody, recording or clip blocking.

## Pilot version: 45-60 seconds

1. Unanswered messages and CHAR_001 preparing to search.
2. First door: "He was probably at the cafe."
3. Second friend joins with an irrelevant object in hand.
4. Fast montage of theories, coffee and more people joining.
5. The group marches seriously through the neighborhood.
6. Dawn: music leaks from CHAR_002's apartment.
7. Door opens on the after-party. CHAR_002 asks, "Why are all of you here?"

## Production advantages

- One main neighborhood and one apartment interior.
- CHAR_001 remains the visual anchor.
- The growing group can be introduced through short inserts rather than complex crowd shots.
- Comedy can be created in editing, reaction shots and sound design, reducing the need for complicated action generation.
- The final after-party can be built with a small number of repeated background inserts: cups, shoes, sleeping bodies, speaker, kitchen light and doorway reactions.

## Recommended canon change

Replace the previous serious mystery version with this comedic search version for the next screenplay pass. Keep the green house as a recurring neighborhood landmark, but make it one of the wrong locations the group checks before reaching CHAR_002's apartment.
