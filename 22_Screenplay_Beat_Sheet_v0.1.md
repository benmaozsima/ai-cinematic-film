# Screenplay Beat Sheet v0.1

Project: The Visitor in the Green House
Status: DESIGNED
Target runtime: approximately 6:30

## Dramatic promise

The film begins like a gentle adult version of a familiar children's-song ritual. It becomes a realistic mystery about guilt, and ends with one simple action that proves the protagonist has stopped hiding inside the ritual.

## Scene outline

### Scene 1 - Preparation (0:00-0:35)

CHAR_001 polishes one shoe, combs his silver-gray hair and places a small envelope inside his jacket. He checks the time, puts on his hat and takes the walking stick. The apartment is neat but too quiet. He pauses at a framed photograph turned slightly away from camera.

Purpose: establish the ritual, the man and the hidden emotional object.

### Scene 2 - The first door (0:35-1:15)

He walks through the old neighborhood to a familiar apartment. He rings once, waits, then rings again. No answer. A neighbor, CHAR_003, opens the adjacent door and recognizes him. She says the friend is not there and gives him another address.

Purpose: make the visit structure clear and create the first human connection.

### Scene 3 - The second walker (1:15-1:55)

CHAR_003 joins him for part of the route. They pass the cafe frontage. She mentions that CHAR_002 always sat at the same table but refuses to say why he left. CHAR_001 corrects one small detail, too quickly.

Purpose: introduce conflicting memories and show that CHAR_001 controls the story.

### Scene 4 - The cafe clue (1:55-2:40)

At the cafe, an older waiter places a cup on an empty table without being asked. Under the table is a faded scratch: two short marks, CHAR_002's old habit. CHAR_001 finds a second envelope or a torn piece of paper. He hides it before anyone sees.

Purpose: give the mystery a physical trail and reveal the route is not accidental.

### Scene 5 - The growing route (2:40-3:35)

A third person joins after a brief doorstep exchange. The three walk together. The same street appears from a new angle. The light has changed slightly. CHAR_001 keeps saying they are almost there, although the green house is still not visible.

Purpose: echo the source structure while making repetition increasingly uneasy.

### Scene 6 - The truth leaks out (3:35-4:35)

In the courtyard, the third person says CHAR_002 did not simply disappear. Years earlier, he asked CHAR_001 to testify or speak up during a humiliating public incident. CHAR_001 stayed silent. CHAR_002 left soon afterward. CHAR_001 insists there was nothing he could do, but his hand crushes the envelope.

Purpose: state the adult conflict clearly enough that the audience understands the moral stakes.

### Scene 7 - The group stops (4:35-5:05)

At the edge of the neighborhood, the others stop walking. CHAR_003 says there is no next address. CHAR_001 continues alone. The sound of the group fades, leaving only the cane and his shoes.

Purpose: remove the comforting social ritual and force the protagonist toward a choice.

### Scene 8 - The green house (5:05-6:05)

The green house is exactly as remembered: faded walls, red door, shaded entry. The door is already open. Inside, CHAR_001 finds a small table, the missing half of the envelope and a recording or written message from CHAR_002. The message does not forgive him; it says that returning every day is another way of refusing to arrive.

Purpose: deliver the moral through a concrete confrontation, not an abstract explanation.

### Scene 9 - The break in the ritual (6:05-6:30)

CHAR_001 places the hat and walking stick beside the door. He leaves the envelope on the table and steps outside without looking back. He walks down a different street. From inside the empty house, a doorbell rings once. Cut to black before we know who rang it.

Purpose: show change while preserving a final controlled mystery.

## Moral clarity test

By the end, the viewer must understand three facts:

1. CHAR_001 has been repeating the route to avoid facing his guilt.
2. CHAR_002's disappearance is connected to CHAR_001's silence.
3. CHAR_001 changes only when he stops repeating the route.

## Tone rules

- Serious, restrained and realistic.
- No broad comedy, fantasy creatures or melodramatic shouting.
- Familiarity comes from repeated actions and visual rhythm, not copied lyrics.
- The film should feel affectionate toward the childhood source while clearly becoming its own adult story.

## Open screenplay decisions

- Choose whether the final message is audio, handwriting or both.
- Decide what public incident caused the original betrayal.
- Decide whether CHAR_002 is alive somewhere or permanently absent.
- Decide whether the final doorbell is real, accidental or deliberately ambiguous.

## Gate

Do not write final dialogue until the four open decisions are selected. The beat sheet can be storyboarded in rough form, but paid generation remains blocked until the visual references and shot budget are approved.
