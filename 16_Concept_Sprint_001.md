# Concept Sprint 001

Status: IDEA
Date: 2026-08-28
Purpose: Generate original film directions before approving one for worldbuilding.

## Non-negotiable constraints

- Original story, world, characters, vehicles, locations and visual identity.
- Target length: 5-8 minutes.
- Highest practical cinematic quality, with consistency before spectacle.
- Total production budget target: $100 maximum.
- No paid generation without Ben's explicit approval after a dry-run and cost estimate.
- Prefer a small cast, a limited number of locations and reusable visual anchors.

## Candidate A: The Last Tide Map

**Logline:** In a future where coastlines change every night, a young cartographer follows a living map carved into wet sand to find the village her missing mother erased from history.

**World:** A drowned coastal civilization of black tidal flats, half-submerged observatories and wind-battered shelters. The ocean behaves like a memory: it briefly reveals places connected to strong human attachment.

**Emotional engine:** Grief becoming acceptance. The protagonist must stop trying to recover the past exactly as it was.

**Visual identity:** Blue-black night, silver moonlight, pale bioluminescent water, rusted instruments and warm amber lanterns. Surreal, poetic and meditative rather than militaristic.

**AI production strengths:** One protagonist, one recurring coastline, one prop (the folding tide map), repeatable sand grooves and a clear transformation from night to dawn. Strong first/last-frame opportunities.

**Main risk:** Water and sand continuity can be difficult; keep the geography simple and use the map as the continuity device.

## Candidate B: The Orchard Above the Clouds

**Logline:** A maintenance worker on a floating agricultural station discovers that the last living tree is sending messages through its roots, asking her to shut down the station before the sky-city consumes the land below.

**World:** A quiet vertical city built above permanent cloud cover. Orchards, service tunnels, glass bridges and abandoned weather machinery share the same structure.

**Emotional engine:** Choosing a living future over a comfortable system that has protected the protagonist.

**Visual identity:** White cloud oceans, oxidized copper, translucent glass, deep green leaves and controlled shafts of sunlight. A restrained science-fiction fable with tactile machinery.

**AI production strengths:** A compact location kit, a single hero prop (root-monitoring headset), one tree as a strong reference anchor, and manageable character motion.

**Main risk:** Floating-city scale could become generic. The production bible must lock a distinctive station architecture and a small set of recurring shapes.

## Candidate C: The House That Remembers Rain

**Logline:** In a desert where rain falls only inside abandoned homes, a traveling archivist enters one final house and finds memories of a family that may still be alive in the storm.

**World:** A vast mineral desert dotted with empty houses. Each house contains a different private weather system: mist, rain, hail or warm summer drizzle.

**Emotional engine:** The protagonist learns that preserving memories is not the same as living inside them.

**Visual identity:** Sun-bleached exteriors, dark interiors, rain-lit rooms, faded family objects and reflections that move slightly before the people do. Intimate magical realism.

**AI production strengths:** One primary interior, one exterior, a small cast seen mostly through silhouettes/reflections, and sound-led transitions between weather states.

**Main risk:** Reflections and rain are technically unstable. Use them as short inserts and build the story around closeups, doorways and controlled camera moves.

## Candidate D: The Signal Beneath the Ice

**Logline:** A solitary radio engineer at an Arctic relay station receives a daily transmission from beneath the ice, carrying the voice of a person who has not yet disappeared.

**World:** A remote polar relay station, pressure tunnels and a frozen sea containing a buried pre-human signal array. The signal appears as patterns in frost and sound, not as a visible monster.

**Emotional engine:** The difference between rescuing someone and accepting a warning meant for the future.

**Visual identity:** Cold cyan daylight, sodium-orange interiors, frost crystals, analog radio equipment and vast white silence. Suspense through stillness and precise sound.

**AI production strengths:** Strong recurring props, limited cast, clear radio dialogue, controlled interiors and a natural reason for subtitles and inserts. Good model-routing candidate: image-to-video for character shots, controlled transitions for the ice reveal.

**Main risk:** Arctic imagery is familiar. The buried signal array and the station's unusual circular architecture must define the look.

## Candidate E: Roots of the Blue Forest

**Logline:** A man returns to a vanished forest where braided strands of his hair begin merging with blue pathways in the sand, leading him toward a truth he spent years refusing to remember.

**World:** A symbolic shoreline-forest that appears only at twilight. The ground is carved by waves into organic grooves that behave like roots; a bioluminescent blue forest grows from them.

**Emotional engine:** Reconciliation with a protected part of the self.

**Visual identity:** Deep blue twilight, luminous foliage, wet sand, wind, braided hair and slow circular camera movement. Poetic surrealism with a human face kept natural and recognizable.

**AI production strengths:** One central performer, a small visual vocabulary and a powerful identity anchor. The braided-hair/sand-root motif can unify every shot and translate well into still-first production.

**Main risk:** Symbolic imagery can feel vague without a concrete dramatic objective. The screenplay must give the protagonist a specific object or message to retrieve.

## Candidate F: Adult Reimagining of a Children's Story

This is a valid direction, but it needs a strict adaptation rule: preserve the emotional DNA, not the recognizable surface.

**Method:** Choose a public-domain children's story, identify its central human truth, then rebuild the setting, characters, conflict and visual language for an adult audience in a believable real-world environment.

**Examples of transformation:**

- A lost-child story becomes an adult searching for a sibling after a disaster.
- A talking-animal fable becomes a workplace or family power struggle where the "animals" are human archetypes.
- A journey through a strange land becomes a night crossing through a real border, city or wilderness.
- A fairy-tale promise becomes a debt, a legal obligation, a medical decision or a secret kept for years.

**Production advantage:** Familiar emotional structure gives the short a clear spine, while realistic locations, clothing and props make visual consistency easier than a fully invented fantasy world.

**Creative warning:** Avoid direct names, copied dialogue, signature costumes, iconic visual designs or a scene-by-scene remake. The result must stand on its own as an original film.

**Best candidates to investigate:** public-domain stories with a strong emotional engine and few essential characters, such as *The Little Match Girl*, *The Fisherman and His Wife*, *The Steadfast Tin Soldier*, *The Ugly Duckling* or *The Pied Piper*. Final selection requires checking the exact source version and adaptation distance.

**Possible tonal direction:** grounded adult drama with one impossible element treated as real, using restrained surrealism, naturalistic performances and a documentary-feeling camera language.

## Candidate G: The Visitor in the Green House

**Source relationship:** Free original reimagining inspired by the childlike structure and ritual of the Israeli song "Adon Shoko". This is not a remake and must not use its lyrics, melody, recording, music video or distinctive sequence of scenes without permission.

**Logline:** Every morning, a carefully dressed older man leaves his apartment to visit an old friend. Each visit leads him to another address, until he realizes he is walking through the vanished map of a friendship that ended decades ago.

**Opening language:** Polished shoes, a comb, a hat and a walking stick. A quiet morning. A colored door. The images initially feel gentle and familiar, then the details become subtly wrong: the street is too empty, the addresses repeat, and no one remembers the man he is looking for.

**World:** A recognizable Israeli neighborhood in the present day: aging apartment blocks, small cafes, bus stops, stairwells and one green house with a red door. The realism is important; the uncanny element should remain restrained.

**Emotional engine:** A man uses a daily ritual to avoid accepting that his closest friend died, left, or chose to disappear. The final visit forces him to decide whether to keep repeating the route or enter the empty house.

**Visual identity:** Warm morning light gradually drains toward cool late afternoon. Muted concrete, faded greens, red door, dark brown wood, pale blue shadows and small chocolate-colored details. Locked wardrobe and props create strong continuity anchors.

**AI production strengths:** One protagonist, one neighborhood, a limited prop kit and repeated walking actions. The story can be built from short controlled shots: preparation inserts, tracking walks, doorways, reaction closeups and sound bridges.

**Sound strategy:** Original score and sound design only. Recurring elements can include shoe polish, a comb on a table, a cane on pavement, a distant bus and a doorbell. The sound rhythm can gently echo a children's-song cadence without reproducing the original melody.

**Main risk:** If the references become too literal, it stops being an original film; if they become too subtle, the connection disappears. We should decide later whether this is presented as an unofficial homage or developed only after permission is obtained.
