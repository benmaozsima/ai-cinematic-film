# Film Definition and Pilot Plan

Project concept: Candidate G - The Visitor in the Green House
Status: DESIGNED
Version: v0.1

## What the film is

This is a serious, realistic adult short inspired by the simple repeating structure of the children's song "Adon Shoko": a man prepares carefully, visits a friend, meets another person, and continues walking. The film begins with gentle familiarity and gradually reveals that the movement has become a way to avoid a painful truth.

It is not a music-video remake. It is an original drama with a recognizable structural homage.

## One-sentence story

An older man follows a chain of visits through his neighborhood in search of a friend who chose to disappear, only to discover that the route was designed to make him confront the betrayal that ended their friendship.

## Moral center

**A life can look full because it is full of activity, while still avoiding the one truth that needs to be faced. Repeating a ritual is not the same as living.**

The ending should not preach this message. The audience should feel it when CHAR_001 reaches the green house and must choose between repeating the route and entering the room he has avoided.

## How the audience recognizes the source DNA

Use five clear signals in the opening 30 seconds:

1. A carefully dressed older man prepares for a morning visit.
2. The recurring wardrobe and prop silhouette: polished shoes, hat and walking stick.
3. A calm walk through an ordinary neighborhood toward a colored door.
4. A short, polite exchange at the door, followed by an invitation or reason to continue.
5. A second figure joining the route, creating a growing chain of walkers.

After these signals, the film must develop its own adult conflict. Do not copy the original lyrics, melody, recording, clip blocking or exact sequence of locations.

## Feature of the short

### Act 1 - The ritual (0:00-1:30)

CHAR_001 polishes his shoes, combs his hair, puts on his hat and checks a small envelope in his pocket. He walks to a familiar apartment and rings. No answer. A neighbor, CHAR_002, opens another door and says the friend is not there but may be at the next address. They walk together.

### Act 2 - The chain (1:30-4:30)

Each visit adds a clue and a person from the past. The route moves through a cafe, a stairwell and an old courtyard. The group grows, but the emotional space around CHAR_001 becomes emptier. The visitors remember the friend differently. CHAR_001 keeps correcting them and insisting on the next address.

The hidden conflict emerges: CHAR_001 betrayed the friend years earlier by staying silent when the friend needed him. The daily route is his punishment and his excuse.

### Act 3 - The green house (4:30-6:30)

The group stops following. CHAR_001 reaches the green house alone. The red door is open. Inside is the envelope's missing counterpart and a recording or handwritten message that does not forgive him, but tells him to stop returning.

He can either close the door and begin the route again, or enter and accept the end. He enters. The final shot is not a supernatural reveal: it is a quiet physical action that proves he has broken the ritual, such as leaving the hat and stick by the door and walking outside without them.

## Pilot: 45-60 seconds

The pilot should communicate the whole promise without explaining the ending:

- 0:00-0:07: shoe polish, comb, hat, envelope
- 0:07-0:16: measured walk through the neighborhood
- 0:16-0:24: green house and red door
- 0:24-0:31: doorbell; a polite stranger says the friend is elsewhere
- 0:31-0:42: a second person joins the walk; the route begins repeating
- 0:42-0:53: three walkers pass the same street from a changed angle and changed light
- 0:53-1:00: CHAR_001 sees the green house again; cut to black on the doorbell

The pilot's job is to prove character consistency, realistic walking, neighborhood continuity, the repeated-visit grammar and the serious tone. It does not need to solve the mystery.

## Best production method

1. Lock CHAR_001 with a neutral portrait, three-quarter portrait, full-body wardrobe and walking reference.
2. Lock one neighborhood kit: apartment entrance, colored doors, cafe frontage, courtyard and green house.
3. Generate storyboard keyframes for the pilot before any video.
4. Use image-to-video for all character shots, with the same approved character references.
5. Use short clips of 3-5 seconds and edit the walking chain together.
6. Use one controlled transition or first/last-frame shot for the repeated green-house reveal.
7. Record original dialogue and sound effects separately; use recurring footsteps, cane, doorbell and street ambience as continuity motifs.
8. Apply one final grade so every generated source belongs to the same film.

The 2026 workflow research consistently points to reference-first production, storyboard/keyframe planning and short controlled clips as the most reliable approach for narrative consistency. Pre-production is currently stronger and more controllable than long-form generation from one prompt.

## Model routing hypothesis

- Character and location keyframes: one image model and one locked prompt grammar.
- General walking and reaction shots: Seedance 2.5 image-to-video draft candidate.
- Precise green-house arrival or doorway transition: Veo 3.1 first/last-frame candidate.
- Voice and final sound: separate original voice, music and effects workflow.

These are candidates, not approvals. Every paid test still requires a dry-run, cost estimate and Ben's explicit approval.
